# 11 — Filing walkthrough

> **Status:** unverified — research brief, not findings
> **Sources:** none held
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-27

## What this document must establish

The practical path from "I have my figures" to "my return is filed and my tax is paid" —
for someone doing it themselves for the first time.

## Why this is the document users will actually read

Everything else in this repo establishes what is owed. This one establishes what to do
about it. For a first-time self-filer, the obstacle is rarely the arithmetic — it is not
knowing that a TIN is needed, which of the return's schedules apply to them, or what to
do when the portal rejects a submission without explaining why.

## Questions to answer

**Getting registered**

1. Who must register, and by when?
2. How a TIN is obtained — online or in person, what documents, how long it takes.
3. Whether registration for income tax is separate from other tax types.
4. Registering for e-Services, and how that relates to holding a TIN.

**The return**

5. Which return form applies to an individual for Y/A 2025/2026.
6. Whether a simplified filing option exists, who qualifies, and what it omits.
7. **The schedules, one by one**, and which apply to each persona. A consultant with only
   foreign income should be told which three schedules they need, not handed a form with
   twenty.
8. **Where foreign-currency service income is reported**, and where the remittance
   condition is evidenced or declared on the form. If the form has a specific field or
   declaration for this, it is the single most useful fact in this document.
9. Where APIT and WHT credits are claimed.
10. What supporting documents must be attached, and what must merely be retained.

**Paying**

11. Payment channels and what reference is needed.
12. Whether payment and filing are separate acts with separate deadlines.
13. How instalment payments are made and how they are matched to the taxpayer.

**When it goes wrong**

14. Common rejection reasons on the e-Services portal.
15. How to amend a filed return, and the time limit.
16. How to file for a year already missed.

## What the calculator needs from this

- Output mapped to the return's own field names, so a user can transcribe from the tool
  to the form without guessing which box
- A per-persona checklist of the schedules that apply
- The exact wording of any remittance declaration on the form, so the tool's question can
  match the form's question rather than approximating it

## A note on the form as a source

The IIT return form and its guide to completion are, for several purposes, better sources
than the act. The form reveals how IRD expects the computation to run — including,
possibly, **Q14**, the unresolved question of how relief is allocated across rate
schedules. The structure of the form's schedules may answer it directly where the act is
silent.

Worth obtaining early for that reason alone.

## Where to look

The IIT return form and guide for Y/A 2025/2026. IRD e-Services documentation. IRD
notices on e-filing and on registration. IRA 2017 filing and registration provisions for
the underlying obligations.
