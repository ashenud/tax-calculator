# 05 — Foreign currency and service export income

> **Status:** partial — the regime, the rate and the operative wording are verified; the conditions' edges are not
> **Sources:** ir-amendment-act-2-2025.pdf, pn-it-2025-01.pdf
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-28
> **Priority:** highest — this is the document the project exists for

## The rule, as enacted

From 1 April 2025 [Act 2/2025 s.1(2)], income tax exemptions on service exports and
foreign source income were **removed** [PN/IT/2025-01, para 3]. Such income is now
taxable, at a maximum rate of 15% [Act 2/2025 s.3; PN/IT/2025-01, para 2.1(b)] — but only
where a remittance condition is satisfied.

### The operative wording

Reproduced verbatim, because everything turns on it. Two limbs.

**Service exports** — the gains and profits earned or derived from

> "any service rendered in or outside Sri Lanka to any person to be utilized outside Sri
> Lanka, where the payment for such services is received in foreign currency and remitted
> through a bank to Sri Lanka"

[Act 2/2025 s.3; PN/IT/2025-01, para 2.1(b)(i)]

**Foreign sources** — the gains and profits earned or derived

> "from any foreign source where such gains and profits are earned or derived in foreign
> currency and remitted through a bank to Sri Lanka"

[Act 2/2025 s.3; PN/IT/2025-01, para 2.1(b)(ii)]

Both limbs attract a maximum rate of 15% [Act 2/2025 s.3].

### What this wording establishes

- The condition is **real and statutory**, not administrative practice.
- It has two components: the payment must be **received in foreign currency**, *and* it
  must be **remitted through a bank to Sri Lanka**. Foreign currency alone is not enough.
- For the service-export limb, the service must be **utilised outside Sri Lanka** —
  where the service is *rendered* may be in or outside Sri Lanka, so a consultant working
  from home in Colombo for an overseas client is within the limb on that point.

### What it does not establish

The Act does not define "remitted", does not define "bank" for this purpose, and sets no
timing rule. So the following remain **open** — see
[`12-open-questions.md`](12-open-questions.md):

| | |
|---|---|
| Q10 | Must remittance occur within the year of assessment the income arises in? |
| Q11 | Partial remittance — per-payment, apportioned, or all-or-nothing? |
| Q12 | What evidence IRD accepts |
| Q13 | Whether a foreign-currency account held at a Sri Lankan bank qualifies |
| Q41 | Whether Wise / Payoneer / PayPal settling into a local bank is "remitted through a bank" |
| Q9 | The precise consequence of failing the condition |

These are the questions an actual consultant has, and the statute does not answer them.
They need the WHT circular, IRD guidance, or the Y/A 2025/2026 return form.

> **A standing limitation.** Act 2/2025 provides that the **Sinhala text prevails** in
> case of inconsistency with the English. This repository works from the English text.
> Any conclusion resting on fine wording — which is most of this document — carries that
> caveat.

## Why this document matters more than the others

Until 31 March 2025, this income was broadly exempt. From 1 April 2025 it is taxable — at
a reduced rate, but **only where the earnings are remitted to Sri Lanka through a bank**
[Act 2/2025 s.3]. Earnings kept offshore appear to fall back to the full progressive
ladder.

> **A correction worth recording.** Secondary sources almost universally describe this as
> requiring remittance through a **licensed** bank. The statutory words are "remitted
> through a bank to Sri Lanka" — there is no "licensed" qualifier in the text
> [Act 2/2025 s.3]. Whether "bank" is nonetheless a defined term elsewhere in the IRA
> that imports a licensing requirement is **not yet checked**, and until it is, this
> document uses the statutory wording rather than the popular gloss.

That is a large swing in liability turning on a single factual condition, affecting a
population — remote consultants, freelance developers, designers working for overseas
clients — who mostly file their own returns without professional help, and who spent
several years correctly believing this income was exempt.

No consumer calculator asks about the remittance condition. Being the tool that does is
the point of this project.

## Questions to answer

**The regime**

1. What is the reduced rate, and is it a flat rate or a ladder with a cap?
2. Is personal relief available against this income? (Q6 — for a consultant whose only
   income is foreign, this substantially changes the answer.)
3. What is the exact scope: "service export income", "foreign source income", or both as
   separate categories with separate conditions?
4. Are these defined terms? Reproduce the definitions verbatim.

**The condition — the core of this document**

5. **Quote the operative words exactly.** "Remitted through a bank" and "received in Sri
   Lanka through a bank" are not the same test, and the difference decides real cases.
   Paraphrase is not acceptable here.
6. Is **"bank"** a defined term in the IRA, and does the definition import a licensing
   requirement? The statutory words are simply "a bank" — the "licensed bank" gloss is
   secondary-source language. Does a licensed finance company count?
7. **Timing**: must remittance occur within the year of assessment in which the income
   arises? What of income earned in March and remitted in April?
8. **Partial remittance**: where a consultant remits part of their earnings and retains
   the rest abroad — say, an illustrative 60% and 40% — is the condition applied
   per-payment, apportioned, or all-or-nothing? (Q11 — this is the most common real
   pattern and the one most likely to be got wrong.)
9. Does income received into a **foreign-currency account held at a bank in Sri Lanka**
   satisfy the condition? (Q13 — very common arrangement for this population.)
10. What about receipts through payment intermediaries — Payoneer, Wise, PayPal — that
    settle into a local bank account? Does the intermediary break the chain?
11. What evidence does IRD require, and what does it look like in practice?

**Consequences and mechanics**

12. What exactly happens when the condition is not met — the full normal ladder, or
    something else?
13. Is there any election, or is treatment automatic on the facts?
14. Which exchange rate applies, and at what date? (Also raised in
    [`02-assessable-income.md`](02-assessable-income.md).)
15. Is any tax withheld at the point of inward remittance, or is this entirely
    self-assessed?
16. How does this interact with foreign tax already paid — is credit available on top of
    the reduced rate?

**History**

17. The exemption's prior basis and the provision that removed it, with effective dates —
    needed to answer users who remember the exemption and to compute earlier years
    correctly.

## What the calculator needs from this

- A remittance question the user can answer honestly, phrased in terms of what actually
  happened to their money — the arrangements above are the reality, not "did you remit
  through a bank"
- Correct handling of partial remittance, once Q11 is settled
- A contrast pair in the worked examples: identical income, remitted and not remitted
- A guidance page that reaches people searching for this before they file

## Where to look

Act 2/2025 — the charging provision and the First Schedule. PN/IT/2025-01 for IRD's
administrative position, which may be more specific than the act on evidence and timing.
IRA 2017 for the definitions the new provisions rely on, and for the exemption as it
previously stood.

Where the act and the public notice differ, record both. The notice reflects how IRD will
actually administer the provision, which is what a taxpayer experiences.
