# 09 — Terminal benefits

> **Status:** partial — the 2017 definition and tables are verified; currency for Y/A 2025/26 is not established
> **Sources:** ir-act-24-2017.pdf
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-28

## Why this has its own tables

Terminal benefits arrive as a single large sum in one year, representing entitlement
accrued over a working life. Taxed as ordinary employment income they would push decades
of accrual through the top band at once. The Act therefore takes them out of the normal
ladder and charges them on separate, much flatter tables keyed to length of service.

A calculator that treats a retirement gratuity as ordinary employment income will
**overstate the liability substantially** — and the person receiving it is typically newly
out of work and least able to absorb the error.

## What counts as a terminal benefit

The Act lists them exhaustively [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3), as enacted 2017]:

| | |
|---|---|
| (a) | amount received in commutation of a pension [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(a), 2017] |
| (b) | amount received as a retiring gratuity [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(b), 2017] |
| (c) | compensation for loss of office or employment, **under a scheme the Commissioner-General considers uniformly applicable** to all individuals employed by the employer [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(c), 2017] |
| (d) | amount paid at or after retirement from a provident fund **approved by the Commissioner-General**, to the extent it does not represent the person's own contributions [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(d), 2017] |
| (e) | amount from a regulated provident fund not representing employer contributions made before 1 April 1968 and interest thereon, where the employer paid tax at 15% on them [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(e), 2017] |
| (f) | amount paid at or after retirement from the Employees' Trust Fund [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(f), 2017] |

### Two conditions that quietly exclude people

**Limb (c) is not "any redundancy payment".** Compensation for loss of office qualifies
only under a scheme the Commissioner-General considers **uniformly applicable to all
employees of that employer** [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(c), 2017]. An individually negotiated
exit package is not obviously within it. That distinction decides whether a large sum is
taxed on the flat tables or on the full ladder, and it is not something a taxpayer would
think to ask about.

**Limb (d) excludes the employee's own contributions** [IRA Sch.1 para 1(3)(d), 2017] —
which is right in principle, since those were already taxed, but it means the taxable
amount is not the figure on the payment advice.

## The tables

Applied to the total terminal benefit, keyed to the **period of contribution or period of
employment**.

### Where the period is 20 years or less

| Total income from employment | Tax payable |
|---|---|
| Not exceeding Rs. 2,000,000 | 0% [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b)(i), 2017] |
| Exceeding Rs. 2,000,000 but not exceeding Rs. 3,000,000 | 5% of the excess over Rs. 2,000,000 [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b)(i), 2017] |
| Exceeding Rs. 3,000,000 | Rs. 50,000 plus 10% of the excess over Rs. 3,000,000 [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b)(i), 2017] |

### Where the period is more than 20 years

| Total income from employment | Tax payable |
|---|---|
| Not exceeding Rs. 5,000,000 | 0% [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b)(ii), 2017] |
| Exceeding Rs. 5,000,000 but not exceeding Rs. 6,000,000 | 5% of the excess over Rs. 5,000,000 [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b)(ii), 2017] |
| Exceeding Rs. 6,000,000 | Rs. 50,000 plus 10% of the excess over Rs. 6,000,000 [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b)(ii), 2017] |

The longer-service table is materially more generous [IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(b), 2017]: its
nil band is more than double that of the shorter-service table. Length of service is therefore a
required input, not a nicety.

> **Currency warning.** These are the 2017 tables. The First Schedule has been amended
> repeatedly and re-lettered since — see the warning in
> [`07-wht-ait-and-credits.md`](07-wht-ait-and-credits.md). Whether these thresholds still
> stand for Y/A 2025/2026 is **not established**; neither Act 2/2025 nor PN/IT/2025-01
> mentions terminal benefits. Recorded as Q32.

## How they interact with the rest of the computation

The Act's structure is explicit: where an individual's taxable income includes terminal
benefits, capital gains, or the specially-rated business income, those components are
taxed at their own rates and

> "only the remainder of the individual's taxable income shall be taxed at the rates
> referred to in subparagraph (1)"

[IRA Sch.1 para 1(2)(d), as enacted 2017]

**This is the Act's own pattern for carving out a specially-rated component**, and it is
directly relevant to Q14 — how the 15% cap [IRA Sch.1 para 1(6), ins. Act 2/2025 s.3(1)(d)]
on foreign-currency income interacts with the normal ladder. It does not settle Q14: paragraph 1(6) is drafted
as a *maximum rate* and carries no equivalent "only the remainder" limb. But it shows how
the drafter handles the same structural problem elsewhere in the same paragraph, which is
evidence worth having. See [`12-open-questions.md`](12-open-questions.md).

## Still unestablished

- Whether the tables above are current for Y/A 2025/2026 (Q32)
- Whether the employer must deduct at the point of payment, and at what rate
- Whether the tax is final or the benefit is reported on the return with a credit
- Whether any cumulative or lifetime limit applies across multiple terminal benefits from
  different employers over a career
- How the tables interact with personal relief
- How "period of contribution or period of employment" is measured across broken service
